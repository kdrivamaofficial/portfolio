💬 Chat simulator

A scored live-chat simulator for handling difficult customers.

What it trains

Synchronous chat handling under time pressure. Two scenarios, both drawn from real e-commerce complaint patterns:

  • Sarah Johnson — wrong item received. Ordered one model, received another, wants a replacement and compensation.
  • Michael Chen — return rejected. Sent back a damaged item, was refused a refund, and escalates hard. This scenario uses strong language by design.

What it scores

Greeting speedA 15-second window to open the chat before the customer disengages
Empathy and probingAcknowledging the emotion, then obtaining the order ID and supporting evidence
Hold disciplineResponding before the inactivity clock runs out
Escalation callEscalating only when the customer actually asks, or when the fix sits outside the agent’s authority
ClosingReconfirming the resolution and signing off properly

The running score and the service-level clocks sit in the right rail, so the trainee sees the cost of a slow greeting while it is still happening rather than only in the debrief.

Under the hood

Node and Express, with the customer’s turn produced by DeepSeek and a chain of fallbacks behind it. If every AI provider is unavailable, the session drops to a guided multiple-choice flow — the wording changes, the scoring does not.

The judgement is made on substance rather than keywords: “could you share the reference number” satisfies the order-ID step just as “what is your order ID” does. The interface is modelled on a Salesforce Lightning service console, so the rehearsal matches the software an agent actually sits in.

✉️ Email simulator

A scored email-handling simulator. One hostile case, a response clock, and a four-part quality breakdown with a model answer.

What it trains

Asynchronous written handling — where the agent gets one shot at a reply and cannot read the customer’s reaction before sending.

The case: James Carter is locked out of his account. He pressed Forgot Password, the reset email never arrived, and he opens with an accusation rather than a question. The trainee composes a full email reply in the console, against a response clock. Unlike a chat, there is no room to recover a bad opening line halfway through.

What it scores

Four dimensions, 25 points each:

GreetingNaming the customer and identifying the brand, promptly
EmpathyAcknowledging the lockout and the failed reset before jumping to the fix
SolutionNaming the root cause, reissuing access, and stating what happens next
ClosingInviting confirmation that the problem is actually resolved

On top sit deductions for a slow reply and for an incorrect escalation call, plus a hard fail for conduct that would damage the brand. The escalation step is its own decision: whether the case needs escalating at all, and if so, which queue it routes to.

The debrief

The evaluation panel scores the reply live. “See detailed feedback” opens a per-dimension breakdown — why the score landed where it did, what a full-mark answer would have covered, and a worked example reply to compare against. A score alone tells a trainee they were weak on closing; a model answer shows them what closing well looks like.

K Store is a fictional brand. The customers, orders and evidence are invented for training purposes and are not affiliated with any employer or client.
